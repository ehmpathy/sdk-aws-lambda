## .the dimensions

### D1 — actor (4)

`{ migrant.endpoint-native, migrant.legacy-handler, native, maintainer }` — from the cast above.

**orthogonal because** who runs the util does not constrain what the handler does. either migrant
and a native can each hit a constraint error; each can hit a malfunction.

🔴 **one D1 × D3 dependency, found at peer review: `migrant.legacy-handler` cannot walk the
`wrapped` frame.** the wrapped frame is a `genLambdaEndpoint` concept end to end — `trail` →
`isContempCaller` → the contemp dialect — and **the legacy family has no such switch**
(`--in ehmpathy/simple-lambda-handlers --words 'isContempCaller'` → **0 matches**; it appears in 7
files of this sdk). so a `{ event, trail }` wrapper handed to a `createStandardHandler` handler is
not a frame at all; it is a malformed **event**, which that handler's own zod schema rejects.

⇒ **D1 and D3 are therefore not fully orthogonal**: one actor value bars one frame value. the region
is inapplicable rather than untested — no experience hides there — but the bar is real and the
inventory now marks those 8 cells `n/a-by-nature` rather than counts them walked.

⚠️ **that defends the MECHANICS, and actor varies the CARE — noted at peer review.** under
`define.experience`, care (`freq × cost`) is a native coordinate of an experience, not merely a rank
placed upon it. so the actor genuinely *does* change the experience: nia meets case=7 constantly and
maya never does, though both invoke the same function for the same value. **the inventory's single
care column records the OWNER's care** — the actor for whom the cell is sharpest — rather than a
care that holds for all four. the cells are subsumed on mechanics; the care column is a projection,
and it is labelled as one there.

⚠️ **corrected twice, and the two corrections compound.**

- **r1** found the migrant's *definition* wrong: i had taken the wish at its word that the typical
  migrant's handlers are already `genLambdaEndpoint`. measured adoption said otherwise
  (1 repo / 4 handlers vs 20 repos / 318 sites), which is what produced **F7**.
- **r2** found that r1 fixed the prose and not the **axis**. the corrected fact means there are
  *two* migrants with different preconditions and different boundaries, not one migrant with a
  reworded description. so D1 gains a value.

### D2 — handler outcome (4 values → 5 states)

`{ success, constraint-error, malfunction, introspect{ .granted | .denied } }`

this is the axis the wish's contract section is *about*, and the axis with the discovered split.
verified against the code:

| outcome-state | what the handler does in-process | citation |
|-------|----------------------------------|-----------|
| `success` | returns the validated output | `genLambdaEndpoint.forAskEndpoint.ts:111` |
| `constraint-error` | **returns an envelope** | `genConstraintErrorMiddleware.ts:72`; test at `…forAskEndpoint.test.ts:61-81` |
| `malfunction` | **throws** | `genInternalServiceErrorMiddleware.ts:62`; test at `…forAskEndpoint.test.ts:103-121` |
| `introspect.granted` — `env.access === 'prep'` | returns a json-schema pair | `genIntrospectionMiddleware.ts:56-73` |
| `introspect.denied` — `env.access !== 'prep'` | **throws a `ConstraintError`** that names `prep` | `genIntrospectionMiddleware.ts:49-54`; tests at `…forAskEndpoint.test.ts:234-344` |

🔴 **`introspect` carries a conditional sub-axis, and it went undeclared until i009.** the axis read
`{ …, introspect }` — one value — while the inventory's matrix split it into rows #11/#12 (happy, a
schema pair) and #13/#14 (sharp, a denial envelope). **one axis value, two feels, two observers, two
behaviors, gated by a variable no axis named.** that is exactly the hidden dimension
`rule.require.dimensional-decomposition` asks the walk to surface aloud.

⇒ it is a **conditional sub-axis rather than a fifth dimension**, and the distinction is checkable:
`env.access` is read on **no other path** — `genIntrospectionMiddleware` is its only consumer within
the outcome axis. so it multiplies exactly one D2 value and is inert under the other three. the
product is therefore `4 actors × 5 outcome-states × 2 × 2 = 80`, never a clean `4 × 4 × 2 × 2`.

⚠️ **and the declaration exposed a second defect**: D3 is **live** under `introspect.denied` (the
`ConstraintError` is dialect-switched), which contradicts the blanket "D3 inert under introspect"
i had declared at i005. see the inventory's arithmetic note.

**orthogonal because** the outcome is a property of the handler + input, not of who invokes it or
how the payload is framed — with the one conditional sub-axis above, which is a property of the
*ambient env* rather than of the frame or the actor.

### D3 — payload frame (2)

`{ flat, wrapped }` — the two arms of `LambdaHandlerInput`
(`genLambdaEndpoint.forAskEndpoint.ts:20-37`).

**this axis is the discovery of the decomposition.** i did not have it on the first pass; the
product forced it. once D2 × D3 was written out, the grid showed that D3 *changes the observable
result of D2* — the same `constraint-error` cell yields a different envelope shape per frame
(`genConstraintErrorMiddleware.ts:44-52`). a free-list would have treated "the payload format" as
an implementation detail and missed that it is an **experience** axis.

**orthogonal because** a test author can frame either payload around any handler outcome. the two
do not constrain each other — they *interact*, which is exactly what makes the cross worth a demo
rather than a footnote.

### D4 — event fidelity (2)

`{ wire-safe, wire-hostile }`

does the event survive JSON serde intact?

- **wire-safe** — plain json values
- **wire-hostile** — carries a value the wire would strip: a `Date`, a `domain-objects` class
  instance, an `undefined`, a `Map`

this is the axis the wish's JSON-strip evidence is about. it matters because a handler is validated
by zod (`genZodEventValidationMiddleware`), and zod behaves differently on a live `Date` than on
the ISO string the wire would deliver. a util that skips the strip lets a test pass on an object
aws could never deliver.

**orthogonal because** fidelity is a property of the event object the author constructs — the author
may set it freely, whatever the actor, outcome, or frame. no combination is *forbidden*.

⚠️ **but it is not orthogonal in EFFECT, and the distinction is owed — corrected at peer r2.**
D4 is freely *settable* under every D2 state; it is only *observable* under two of the five. for
`malfunction`, `introspect.granted`, and `introspect.denied`, both D4 values yield the identical
experience — a thrown error, a schema dump, and an access denial do not vary by whether the input
carried a `Date`.

⇒ so **D4 is inert under three of D2's five states**, which means D2 partially implies D4's
relevance. an axis pair can be independent in what it *admits* and dependent in what it *reveals*;
this pair is the second. the inventory marks those cells inert rather than walked, and the count
below says so.

### D5 — run boundary (2, with a conditional sub-axis)

`{ referenced, serialized{ .local | .cloud } }`

**how the shapes cross into the endpoint.** declared by the wisher on 2026-09-08, and briefed at
`.agent/repo=.this/role=any/briefs/define.lambda-endpoint-run-boundary.md`.

- **referenced** — you hold the handler function; the event crosses as a live object reference
- **serialized** — you hold the endpoint's slug; the event crosses as json, over a wire that is
  either real (`.cloud`) or simulated in-process (`.local`)

**orthogonal to D1, D3, D4** — the boundary is the caller's choice of *how to reach* the endpoint,
so it constrains no actor, no frame, no fidelity. any of those three may be set freely on either
side.

⚠️ **it is NOT orthogonal to D2, and the walk below proves it.** `serialized` makes a sixth
outcome-state (`resolution-failure`) reachable that `referenced` bars by nature — you cannot fail
to look up a handler you already hold. so the outcome value **set** differs across D5's two values,
and any count that treats the halves as identical is wrong. the per-half derivation below says so
explicitly.

⇒ this is the same correction D4 already carries: an axis pair can be orthogonal in what it
*admits* and dependent in what it *reveals*. a blanket *"constrains no outcome"* would have been
falsified two sections down, in this same file.

🔴 **and it is the most CAUSAL axis in the product** — it predicts the error stance, which no other
axis does:

| boundary | a `constraint-error` arrives as | why |
|---|---|---|
| `referenced` | a **returned** envelope | you are the **host** — you see what the function returned |
| `serialized` | a **thrown** error | you are a **caller** — callers get caller semantics |

the incumbent states this out loud in its own comments: `invokeLambdaForTestingLocally.ts:78`
(*"make the response look like what aws-lambda would return"*) and `:99`, which converts the
returned envelope into a thrown `LambdaInvocationError` — **on the local locus**. so the
conversion follows the **boundary**, never the locus.

**the sub-axis is conditional, exactly as `env.access` is under `introspect`.** `at` exists only
under `serialized`, because `referenced × cloud` is **barred by nature**: a function reference
cannot cross a process. the bar is unrepresentable rather than policed — `onReferenced` takes no
`at` parameter, so the impossible cell cannot be written
(`rule.prefer.prevent-over-correct`, rung 1).

**and `at` is inert for the CONTRACT.** `.local` and `.cloud` both throw, both strip, both wrap.
they diverge only in their **setup** failure modes — an absent `serverless.yml` or an unresolvable
handler path (six throw sites at `invokeLambdaForTestingLocally.ts:23,37,44,51,65,73`) versus
absent credentials or an undeployed function. ⇒ that is a **sixth outcome-state**
(`resolution-failure`) that D2 does not carry, and it is named in the yield rather than absorbed
silently.

⚠️ **this axis was absent from the r1–i013 walk, and its absence hid the wish's scope.** every one
of the 80 cells was implicitly at `referenced` — the in-process util the wish asked for. the
`serialized` half is the **519-site acceptance surface** the yield had deferred to Q10 with an
unverified *"`askLambdaEndpoint` already ships"*. once the axis is declared, that deferral reads as
what it is: **half the product, unwalked.**

### D6 — event source (6)

`{ ask, apiGateway, sqs, sns, kinesis, s3 }`

**what wakes the endpoint, and therefore what envelope the event arrives in.** briefed at
`.agent/repo=.this/role=any/briefs/define.lambda-event-source.md`.

⚠️ **this axis was absent until i015, and two peer reviewers raised its absence as a blocker.** the
wisher ruled all three export families in scope (wish **A2**), and the third family — the event
factories, **8 repos / 254 sites** — construct exactly this envelope. an in-scope family with no
axis is a real path off a missing axis.

| source | envelope | the payload lives at |
|---|---|---|
| **ask** | none — the event IS the payload | the event itself |
| **apiGateway** | `{ body, headers, httpMethod, path, requestContext, … }` | `body`, as a json string |
| **sqs** | `{ Records: [{ body, messageId, … }] }` | `Records[].body`, as a json string |
| **sns** | `{ Records: [{ Sns: { Message, … } }] }` | `Records[].Sns.Message` |
| **kinesis** | `{ Records: [{ kinesis: { data, … } }] }` | `Records[].kinesis.data`, base64 |
| **s3** | `{ Records: [{ s3: { bucket, object, … } }] }` | the object ref — there is no payload |

**orthogonal to D1 and D5, and NOT to D2, D3, or D4.** the blanket claim *"any source, at either
boundary, in either frame, at either fidelity"* was asserted at i015 and is **false on two of the
three axes** — walked here rather than left as an assertion:

| pair | verdict | why |
|---|---|---|
| D6 × **D1** (actor) | ✅ orthogonal | every actor constructs every source. no cell is barred |
| D6 × **D5** (boundary) | ✅ orthogonal | a constructed event crosses either boundary — `onReferenced({ event, handler })` and `onSerialized({ which, event })` both take one |
| D6 × **D3** (frame) | 🔴 **inert for 4 of 6** | the trail wrapper wraps the **payload**. for `ask` the payload IS the event, so the frame is live. for `apiGateway` it is the `body` string — live, but nested. for `sqs`/`sns`/`kinesis` the payload is a **string inside a Record**, and `s3` carries no payload at all — so a `{ event, trail }` wrapper has nowhere to sit and `getIsWrappedPayload` never fires |
| D6 × **D4** (fidelity) | 🔴 **inert for 4 of 6** | wire-hostility is a property of a **live object** the strip must flatten. `sqs`/`sns`/`kinesis` payloads are already **json strings** by the time the author writes them, and `s3` carries none — a `Date` cannot survive into them to be stripped. live for `ask`, and for `apiGateway` only if the author passes a non-string `body` |
| D6 × **D2** (outcome) | 🔴 **rewrites the value set** | see the table below |

⇒ **so the cross-product is far smaller than a `6 × 2 × 2` suggests**: only `ask` and `apiGateway`
carry live frame and fidelity axes, and `ask` is the slice the 176-cell walk already covers in full.
what D6 genuinely adds beyond that walk is `apiGateway`'s cross-cells plus four sources whose
frame/fidelity axes are inert by construction.

⚠️ **the four inert cells are a finding, not an omission** — an sqs payload is a string because sqs
delivers strings, so the strip has no live object to flatten and the wrapper has no slot to nest in.
that is the same shape as D4's inertness under `malfunction`: an axis that is *admissible*
everywhere and *observable* in few places.

🔴 **NOT orthogonal to D2, and the dependency runs one way.** the source selects which **endpoint
kind** can accept the event, and the endpoint kind rewrites D2's value set outright:

| source | endpoint kind | what a `constraint-error` looks like |
|---|---|---|
| `ask` | `forAskEndpoint` | a returned envelope (`genConstraintErrorMiddleware.ts:72`) |
| `apiGateway` | `forApiGateway` | `{ statusCode: 400, body }` (`:55-67`) — and a **malfunction returns a 500 rather than a throw** (`genInternalServiceErrorMiddleware.ts:39-58`) |
| `sqs`/`sns`/`kinesis`/`s3` | a consumer handler | void, or batch-item-failures |

⇒ so D6 sits **upstream** of D2 and cannot be a parameter of it. the whole D1×D2×D3×D4×D5 product
walked above is the slice at **D6 = `ask`** — the one source `genLambdaEndpoint.forAskEndpoint`
serves, and the one the wish scopes its invoke half to.

**that is the seam with the out-of-scope axis declared below**: *event construction* is in scope for
all six sources, because the wisher ruled the factory family in. *endpoint kind* stays out of scope,
because the wish scopes its **invoke** half to `genLambdaEndpoint`. the factory emits an envelope;
what accepts it is a separate wish.

## .the axis this decomposition does NOT cover — declared, not hidden

> ⚠️ **found at self-review r1, after the product was walked.** recorded here rather than
> back-fitted, because an undeclared gap is worse than a declared one.

there is a fifth candidate axis — **endpoint kind** `{ forAskEndpoint, forApiGateway }` — and this
decomposition covers only the first value.

the evidence it is real: consumer tests use the legacy util on **api-gateway** handlers too, and
assert an http-shaped response rather than a domain output:

```ts
// ahbode/svc-gateway/…/corsPreflightResponse.integration.test.ts:7
const { statusCode, headers } = await invokeHandlerForTesting<any>({ … });
// ahbode/svc-app-usage-events/…/apiCaptureEvents.integration.test.ts:10
const { statusCode, body } = await invokeHandlerForTesting<any>({ … });
```

`forApiGateway` is a real export of this sdk (`index.ts:45`), and its error middlewares behave
**differently** from the ask-endpoint ones — `genConstraintErrorMiddleware.ts:55-67` returns a
`{ statusCode: 400, body }` http response, and `genInternalServiceErrorMiddleware.ts:39-58`
**returns a 500 rather than a throw**. so the D2 outcome axis takes entirely different values under
that endpoint kind, which is precisely what makes it a separate axis rather than another value.

**why it is out of scope here, not merely missed**: the wish scopes its **invoke** half to
`genLambdaEndpoint`. an api-gateway variant is a real follow-on need, but it would **double the
product again** and change the util's return contract. raised as **Q6** in the yield for the wisher
to scope, rather than absorbed silently.

⚠️ **do not confuse this with D6.** D6 = `apiGateway` is **in** scope — it names the *envelope a
factory constructs*, and the wisher ruled the factory family in (wish **A2**). the endpoint kind
that *accepts* that envelope is what stays out. one wish emits the event; another decides what
receives it.

### 🔴 out of scope to BUILD, in scope to WALK — and case=11 is why the distinction is owed

**the exclusion above is a product-surface exclusion, never a demonstration exclusion.** this
decomposition does not commit to ship `forApiGateway`'s invoke half; it does **walk** that endpoint
kind's behavior, because the walk is what proves D6 is not orthogonal to D2.

⇒ so D6's one critipath demo, [case=11](1.vision.experience.case=11.the-fixture-manufactures-the-defect.md),
runs a `forApiGateway` handler on purpose. its `[t2]` (*"the response is `{ statusCode: 400 }`,
never a throw"*) and `[t4]` (*"a malfunction … RETURNS a 500 … this differs from `forAskEndpoint`,
which throws"*) are **evidence for the non-orthogonality**, not a contract this wish delivers:

| the `forApiGateway` handler in case=11 | verdict |
|---|---|
| the **vehicle** that exercises the in-scope event factory | ✅ the demo's subject |
| the **evidence** that endpoint kind rewrites D2's value set | ✅ why D6 sits outside the product |
| a **commitment** to ship `onReferenced` support for that kind | 🔴 **no** — that is Q6, a follow-on |

⚠️ **without this line, the decomposition contradicts its own flagship D6 demo.** a reader who
cross-checks the two finds the scope note say *"`forApiGateway` is out of scope"* and then finds
case=11 assert that very endpoint kind's outcome semantics. both are correct, on two different
senses of *scope* — and a decomposition that leaves a reader to infer which sense applies has not
declared its bound.

## .what the walk surfaced

three findings a free-list would have missed — each is why the walk was worth the cost:

1. **D2 is not uniform (→ F1).** the free-list version of this axis was one value, "error", because
   the wish describes error treatment as one behavior. a split by *what the handler actually does
   in-process* revealed that `constraint-error` returns and `malfunction` throws. that split is the
   whole contract.

2. **D3 is an experience axis, not an implementation detail (→ F3).** the interaction cell
   `constraint-error × flat` vs `constraint-error × wrapped` produces two different envelope
   shapes from one handler and one input. this is the sharpest edge in the wish and it has no
   mention in the wish text.

3. **a latent axis under D4: who owns the strip.** the `wire-hostile` column asks a question the
   other axes cannot: is the strip the *util's* job or the *author's*? if the util strips, it is
   faithful to the wire but silently mutates the author's object. if it does not, the author can
   write a test that could never pass in production. the column has no comfortable cell — which is
   the tell that it is a genuine design boundary, and it is recorded as such in the yield's
   awkwardness section.
